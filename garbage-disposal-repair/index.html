Garbage disposal repair means finding why the unit stopped, whether it is a jammed flywheel, a tripped overload, a leaking seal or a clog downstream, and fixing it, or replacing the unit when repair no longer pays.

Disposal leaking, jammed or dead: what to do first

Safety first. These steps keep your hands and your cabinet safe.

Switch it off, then cut the power

Turn the wall switch off, then unplug the disposal under the sink or switch off its breaker. Never rely on the wall switch alone.

Never put a hand in

Even a stopped disposal can spring free. Use tongs or pliers to remove anything you can see, and only after the power is off.

Stop the leak path

Put a bucket or container under the unit, shut off the dishwasher, and stop running the sink.

Check the dishwasher

A disposal that leaks at the side hose or backs up into the dishwasher has a drain connection problem.

Photograph the damage

Cabinet floors and drawers soak up leaks fast. Take pictures for your insurer.

Wiring a disposal and the electrical code

A disposal is a plumbing fixture with a motor, so two codes meet at the sink. The City of Hanahan lists the 2020 National Electrical Code (NEC) with South Carolina modifications next to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

That is why a new disposal should be wired to code, not just plugged in wherever it reaches. Ask the City of Hanahan Building and Codes department what applies to your kitchen.

Sources: City of Hanahan, Building Codes and SC LLR, Building Codes in Effect for South Carolina. Checked October 2026.

Humming, silent, or running without grinding: what each symptom means

A humming disposal with no spinning means the flywheel is jammed and the motor is trying to turn. A silent disposal with no hum usually means a tripped overload or no power. A disposal that runs but does not grind has lost its impellers or flywheel connection.

A hum is the classic jam. Cut the power, shine a light in, and remove what you can with tongs. Most units have a hex socket at the center bottom where a wrench turns the flywheel by hand, which frees many jams. Then press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit and test with water running.

Reset button and the safe un-jam routine

The one thing a homeowner can try, and only with the power off.

  1. 1

    Power off

    Wall switch off, then unplug the unit or turn off the breaker.

  2. 2

    Look with a light

    Look down the drain for a bottle cap, bone, utensil or fruit pit.

  3. 3

    Remove with tongs

    Use long-handled tongs or pliers, not fingers.

  4. 4

    Turn the flywheel

    Insert a hex wrench in the bottom center socket and work it back and forth until it spins freely.

  5. 5

    Reset and test

    Press the red button on the bottom, restore power, run cold water and test.

When the sink drain is the real problem

A disposal shares its trap and drain line with the kitchen sink, and often with the dishwasher. A clog downstream looks like a bad disposal: water backs up when it runs, or it gurgles and the sink fills. Before you replace a working unit, rule out the drain.

Greasy water, starchy foods and coffee grounds build up in kitchen drains. Our drain cleaning service clears the line when water backs up past the disposal. Rivers Avenue is Hanahan's main commercial strip, and commercial kitchens along it are a different job from a home sink, which our commercial plumbing work covers.

Dishwasher drain connection: the overlooked cause

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small inlet on the side of the disposal. On a new disposal, that inlet has a plastic knockout plug that must be punched out. If it is left in place, the dishwasher cannot drain, and dirty water sits in the tub. It is a common installation mistake and an easy fix.

A leak at that fitting is often just a loose clamp or a worn hose. If the hose is kinked or has no high loop under the counter, drain water can flow back. We check the connection each time we work on a disposal.

Repair or replace: how we decide

A jam, a tripped overload, a loose flange or a worn hose are repairs. A leak from the body, a seized motor, a noisy bearing or a unit that has been replaced several times in a decade are replacements. Disposals commonly last about 8 to 15 years, and an old budget model costs little compared with repeated service calls.

Horsepower matters. A one-half horsepower unit is fine for light use, while three-quarter or one horsepower units handle larger households and tougher scraps. We recommend a size and tell you what the new mounting and electrical work will take.

Installation basics: what a replacement involves

A swap on the same sink takes a short visit when the old mount is sound.

  1. 1

    Power and water off

    Unplug or switch off the breaker, and clear the cabinet under the sink.

  2. 2

    Disconnect the old unit

    Detach the discharge pipe and dishwasher hose, then twist the unit off the mounting ring.

  3. 3

    Check the sink flange

    Clean the old putty, and reseat the flange with fresh plumber's putty if it is leaking.

  4. 4

    Mount and wire

    Attach the new unit, connect the power cord or hardwire per its instructions, and punch out the dishwasher knockout if used.

  5. 5

    Reconnect and test

    Attach the discharge pipe and hose, run water, check every joint for leaks, and test the reset.

What not to put down a disposal

These cause most jams and clogs.

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the drain.
  • Coffee grounds, eggshells in quantity and rice or pasta that swell.
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks.
  • Fibrous scraps like celery and onion skins in large amounts.
  • Non-food items such as bottle caps, utensils and sponges.
  • Chemical drain cleaners, which can damage seals and are hard on pipes.

Run cold water for several seconds before and after you use it.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle garbage disposal emergencies at night or on weekends in Hanahan?

Yes. Our 24/7 dispatch takes leaking and jammed disposal calls, and we offer same-day appointments when available. We tell you how to stop the leak first.

Where is the reset button?

On the bottom of the unit, under the sink. It is usually red. Cut the power first, wait a few minutes, then press it.

Is it safe to put my hand in to clear a jam?

No. Use tongs or pliers with the power off, even if you think it is off. Turning the flywheel from the bottom with a wrench is safer.

Why does my dishwasher back up after a new disposal?

The inlet knockout plug may not have been removed. It blocks the dishwasher drain until it is punched out.

Why is my disposal leaking from the bottom?

Usually a cracked housing or failed internal seal. That is a replacement, not a repair.

How long do garbage disposals last?

About 8 to 15 years depending on the model and use.

Do you install garbage disposals?

Yes, on a new sink or as a replacement. We check the outlet, the dishwasher hose and the trap while we are there.
