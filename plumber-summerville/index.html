What very soft water does and does not do to your plumbing

With total hardness near 25 ppm, Summerville water leaves little mineral scale in a water heater or on a faucet. That is useful, because it means a softener is not something we will try to sell you. Check your own tap if you have questions, or ask your water utility, because areas outside the town limits may be served by a different provider.

What soft water does not prevent is ordinary wear. Rubber washers harden, anode rods in a tank are used up over time, and tanks usually last roughly 8 to 12 years regardless of how soft the water is. A heater near that age that begins to leak from the tank is a replacement, not a scale cleanup.

Pine roots and old trees: why sewer lines clog here

Summerville is known for mature pines and oaks, and the town has protected its trees since an 1847 ordinance. Roots seek moisture and enter sewer joints, so on an older street a repeat clog deserves a camera look. Once inside, they catch paper and waste and cause repeating clogs.

A cable clears the mass for a while, but the roots grow back. A camera shows where they entered, and hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall better than a cable can. If you plan any tree work, ask the Town of Summerville about its rules first and tell your plumber where the line runs.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

Because Summerville is a longer drive, what you do before we get there counts for more.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main shutoff at your house clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find or close it, call Charleston Water System at (843) 727-6800.

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off breakers for wet rooms, only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground, otherwise stay out and call 911 or the electric utility.

Clear the area

Get valuables and rugs up, and set a bucket under any steady drip.

Sewer backup or overflow when the ground is soaked

A line that backs up after a heavy storm needs fast help. This is the sequence to follow.

Stop all water use

Pause laundry, showers and the dishwasher. Water has no place to go.

Find the cleanout

Find the cleanout but leave the cap on while sewage is rising. Opening it can release wastewater into your yard or house. Tell dispatch where it is and we will open it safely.

Keep clear of the spill

Wear gloves and boots, and keep children out of the area.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell us about recent rain, tree work or a recent clog. Those details point to the cause.

What affects the price of sewer work in Summerville

A clear price comes before any work begins. These factors move it.

Root load
A light root intrusion clears quickly, while a mass across several joints takes more passes.
Pipe material and condition
Old clay or cast iron may need repair after clearing, which adds to the job.
Length and access
A reachable cleanout makes the job faster than a line with no access point.
Method
Cabling is less than jetting, and trenchless lining costs more than clearing alone.
Hour and distance
Night calls cost more, and Summerville is the edge of our ring, so travel counts more than in Hanahan.

Homeowners insurance usually covers sudden damage and not root intrusion over years. Ask your carrier about sewer backup coverage before you need it.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Summerville?

We cover all three: 29485, 29483 and 29486. The 29485 code is the largest and our headline ZIP. Nearby, 29456 in Ladson and 29420 in North Charleston also fall in our range, which helps when a call comes from the southern end of town.

Is emergency service available in Summerville?

Yes, emergency dispatch runs 24/7. Summerville is the edge of our normal range, about 15 miles out, so we tell you honestly on the phone what we can do and when. We do not promise arrival times.

Is Summerville water hard? Do I need a softener?

Summerville CPW reports total hardness averaging 25 ppm and a pH of 7.82, which is very soft. Heavy scale is rare, so a softener is not something we would recommend for plumbing protection. If your home is outside the town system, ask your water utility for its report.

Can tree roots really get into my sewer line?

Yes. Roots seek moisture and can enter at joints in a sewer line, then grow into a mass that catches waste. A camera shows entry points, and jetting clears them. Repair or lining keeps them from returning.

Does the 1847 tree law affect my sewer line?

Not directly. Big trees near an older line are a common root source, so ask the Town of Summerville what tree rules apply before any removal, and tell us where the line runs.

How do I confirm your plumber is licensed?

Check the individual with the SC Residential Builders Commission at LLR. Ask for the name of the plumber who will come, and verify it before work starts.
